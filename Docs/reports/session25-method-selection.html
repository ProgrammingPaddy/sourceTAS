The session-22 ordinal numbering ("capability 1") is retired and has been purged from the design documents and from every suite output label (for example, the test formerly printed as capability-1 gate (build) now prints as A6 band build gate, where A6 is the N-tick air turn/gain maximum). Explanations in reports and chat now define each term where it first appears.

02The one-tick law, in plain language

Everything in the air family reduces to one per-tick rule. Each tick, the player chooses a wish direction. Let α be the angle between that wish and the current velocity, s the current horizontal speed, and define c = s·cos α — the part of the current velocity that points along the wish. With a full stick, the engine then adds exactly a = min(30 − c, budget) units of velocity along the wish (budget = 562.5 × surface-friction; surface friction only matters for hard braking). Two consequences follow by algebra:

change in s² this tick  =  900 − c²    (cap-limited regime)
heading change this tick  =  atan2(a·sin α, s + a·cos α)
One scalar, c, controls both. Gain is maximal at c = 0 (wish exactly perpendicular to velocity): +900 to s² per tick. Turning harder than the free rate costs speed quadratically. This law already existed in code (Strafe::TickLaw) with a 168-row measurement; this session added a one-million-state certificate: across random speeds 2–3400 u/s, all headings, and random wish actions, the closed form matches the bitwise-exact kernel to worst |Δ(s²)| = 5.0 (float-rounding scale — s² itself is ~10⁷) and worst heading deviation 1.7×10⁻⁵ radians (~0.001°). Suite: capkern, now 5 checks, all passing.

03"Let speed be determined by the path" — formalized

Because one scalar controls both speed change and turn, you can eliminate it: given the current speed and the turn you demand this tick, the speed change is determined. A path (a heading sequence) therefore induces its speed profile exactly, tick by tick. Path and speed are not independent unknowns — exactly as conjectured. The most useful special case:

The maximum-gain trajectory family

Hold c = 0 every tick (wish perpendicular to velocity). Then, provably and measured:

  • the speed profile is fixed: s after n ticks satisfies s² = s₀² + 900·n — this is the certified ceiling from session 24, and the built surfaces sit on it exactly;
  • the per-tick turn magnitude is fixed: atan(30/s) — about 2.9° at 600 u/s, shrinking as speed grows;
  • the per-tick step length is fixed (post-update speed × 0.015 s), so every maximum-gain flight of N ticks has the same arc length;
  • the only freedom left is the turn sign each tick, and signs may flip at most once per 6 ticks (the dwell rule). "Straight" motion is the minimum-curvature motif — signs alternating every 6 ticks (measured cost: the reach sweep's straight-line distance came within 1.0 unit of the theoretical arc length).

So the whole family is parameterized by its reversal times — and the certified A6 surfaces measured that optimal flights use at most three reversals. A trajectory space of 43⁹⁰ raw control choices collapses to one, two, or three numbers.

04The point-to-point solver design (registry A12, with A10/A11/A13 as query modes)

Problem: from an exact start state, reach a target point (or a face position at a given tick), producing an exact input schedule, in minimum ticks. Vertical motion is a closed form, so a 3-D target fixes a small window of candidate arrival ticks; each candidate makes the problem two-dimensional. The selected construction:

  1. Feasibility in constant time. Target beyond the certified travel bound (the max-speed integral), or a required terminal speed above √(s₀² + 900N), is impossible — with a proof. These prunes cost nanoseconds and are the certified side of the pair.
  2. Closed-form initial guess. Integrating the maximum-gain law continuously gives endpoint position as a function of the reversal times in closed form; invert it analytically.
  3. Exact polish. Newton's method on the reversal times, where every residual evaluation is one exact rollout through the bitwise-certified kernel (90 ticks ≈ 5.6 microseconds at the measured 16.0M ticks/second). Expected 2–5 rollouts.
  4. Exact hit. The last one or two ticks leave the perpendicular wish free (two scalar unknowns, two endpoint equations) to absorb sub-step error — the returned schedule lands on the target and replays through the engine exactly.
