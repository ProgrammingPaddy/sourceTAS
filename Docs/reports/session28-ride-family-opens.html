01The contact-epsilon discovery

The first build's witnesses failed 10 of 10, and the per-tick diagnostic found the engine at zero contacts on a tick where the rider was pressing into the face at 1.80 u/s. The explanation is a real, previously unstated piece of ride physics:

the rider hovers one trace epsilon (1/32 unit) off the plane — a tick re-contacts only when the inward velocity closes that gap within one tick interval:
v·n · dt ≤ −1/32, i.e. v·n ≤ −2.083 u/s at 66.67 ticks/second.
Inward velocities in the band (−2.08, 0) produce HOVER ticks: legal, airborne, un-clipped. With the stay-on-face guard using this exact condition, all 30 witness replays across three ramp angles became bit-identical continuations of real engine runs. The band also explains the falsifier's ambers (below): trajectories that surf the exact contact boundary, alternating micro-hovers with contact, can beat the pure-contact surface — mixed ride/air motion the conservative guard excludes. The exact boundary condition (carrying the gap as state) is the named refinement.

A second lesson came free: the first entry attempt hit the ramps nearly head-on and lost almost everything to the board clip (settling at 80–140 u/s) — boarding must arrive along the slope, the way real runs do. The suite now enters along the downslope with a small inward push, and the captured start states ride at 450+ u/s.
