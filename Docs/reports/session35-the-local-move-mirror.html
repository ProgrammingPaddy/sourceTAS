01A24 — the census refuted the pretty design, so the mirror won

The first draft shipped two analytical transfer laws — the sequential rebase and the two-plane crease resolution, both transcribed from the verified mover. Then the valley-wedge census reported what actually happens at a crease.

Of 36 crossing ticks, 17 were three-plus-bump chains — clip the left wall, rebase across a partial move, clip the right wall, then resolve a two-plane set that includes the left wall again. No pairwise law covers that. The structural answer: CapEdge::TryMoveLocal — the verified TryPlayerMove transcribed operation-for-operation as a pure local function, with every trace answered by the bitwise-certified A14 clip. Partial-move rebases, the crease projection (of the last clip result, a float-level detail), the stop-dead guard, the mid-loop allsolid zeroing, and the unswept stuck guard all ride along. The gate: 36/36 crossing ticks matched bitwise in both position and velocity, two-bump and three-plus-bump chains alike. The two analytical laws remain in the package as the documented decomposition; the mirror is the executable one.

Two geometry lessons paid for in falsifier time: a valley is non-convex air — its solid is a union of two wall bands, two brushes, never one (the single-brush draft produced a ridge and a rider that stalled at the crease); and finite test brushes matter — an infinite backing slab behind an "opposing ramp" quietly engulfed the entire spawn region, caught only because the clean-path class measured zero.

02A28 — solved paths against real geometry, both verdicts exact

Corridor traversal is the A12 + A14 composition: solve a point-to-point schedule, then certify the flight against nearby geometry without touching the engine.

CapContact::FirstContactOnPath kernel-rolls the schedule and clips every motion segment against the corridor's local set, returning clean, or the obstruction (tick, brush, fraction), or DECLINE outside its domain. Against a two-segment ramp world with solved paths threading the lane: 24/24 agree with full engine replays — 16 clean tick-for-tick, 8 obstructed at the exact tick against the exact brush, at 4–5 µs per path check. The A14 flight gate also gained the stuck-guard predictor: the engine's silent freeze (swept-clean tick, embedded endpoint, velocity zeroed to exactly (0, 0, −6) with no contact event — the recorded d34 pathology) is now predicted from the unswept endpoint query and verified when it fires.

03The sf domain law — found by a 250 u/s divergence in open air

Two flights diverged at tick 2, hundreds of units from any brush, with identical vertical state. The horizontal-only signature pointed at the wish budget.

The air kernel's context carries a fixed surface friction; the engine's is stale-stateful — 0.25 on rising ticks, applied one tick late. A max-gain wish never notices (its accel request, ~30, sits far below either budget), but a braking wish requests hundreds — and 562.5·sf differs by 4× between the two. Measured: ~250 u/s of divergence in a single rising braking tick. The law is now in the terms table where every kernel-roll consumer (A11–A13, A28, C7) can see it: kernel rolls with braking wishes are valid on falling flights, or must carry sf state. The flight gates respect the domain; nothing in the certified suites was outside it — the C7 exactness results stand on their own engine-verified gates.
