01What the misses were made of

The sufficiency falsifier names its misses precisely: random braked schedules from 1400 u/s over 30 ticks end slow and close — deep-interior endpoints the reversal-plus-straight-brake family cannot reach.

The anatomy: reaching those points wants a hard early brake into the slow regime — where turning is fast — then reversals. The family enumerated brakes and reversals, but never reversals placed after the brake run. Session 38 added that shape class, made the brake strength a per-candidate continuous parameter (stage-1 seeds at 0.9 and 0.6, strength climbs in refinement — the A9 range lever), and widened the refinement list to eight candidates for the larger family.

02The rescue tail — and the two measurements that shaped it

Shapes got candidates close (residuals 1–10u); closing to 0.5u was the tail's job, and the tail needed a third control.

The finisher: a 3-parameter least-norm Newton — (tail c1, tail c2, brake strength) against the 2-D endpoint, stepping by Jᵀ(JJᵀ)⁻¹r. Run everywhere, it took the hard cell to 92 — and broke long-N cells the 2×2 already owned (400/N90 went 100 → 91: the strength axis adds linearization error where it isn't needed). Gated as a rescue — firing only after the three proven 2×2 starts leave a brake candidate above 0.5u — it keeps every long-N cell at its old level while holding most of the short-N gain.

The second negative result matters more: post-brake tail-sizing — the exact change that fixed A9's converging-to-nothing tails — regressed this solver (86/68/52 on the N30 cells). In SolveFixedN's flow the overlapping tail is not a bug but a useful hybrid shape dimension, because stage 2 ranks candidates with kernel evaluations that already include it. Same lesson, opposite conclusions in two solvers — only measurement could tell them apart. Both results are recorded on the row.
